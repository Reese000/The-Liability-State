## The Pressure Valve

### The Problem

Some humans need:
- Physical struggle
- Real risk
- Genuine challenge
- Harsh environment

A comfortable post-scarcity world doesn't provide this.

### The Solution

Space and extreme environments serve as **frontiers** for the restless.

## Frontier Functions

### Function 1: Purpose for the Ambitious

| Type of Person | Frontier Role |
|----------------|---------------|
| Risk-takers | Colonization pioneers |
| Builders | Habitat construction |
| Explorers | Survey missions |
| Leaders | Colony governance |

### Function 2: Export Discontent

Historical pattern:
- Restless, aggressive people emigrate to frontier
- Metropolitan areas become more peaceful
- Frontier absorbs revolutionary energy

**Effect**: Social stability in homeland.

### Function 3: Resource Acquisition

| Resource | Source |
|----------|--------|
| Rare metals | Asteroid mining |
| Energy | Solar collection |
| Living space | Orbital habitats |
| Backup | Existential risk insurance |

## Liability in Space

### The Challenge

How does strict liability work off-Earth?

| Situation | Complication |
|-----------|--------------|
| Habitat accident | Who has jurisdiction? |
| Resource dispute | Common heritage vs. property |
| Environmental damage | No one to sue on behalf of Mars |

### Proposed Approach

**Contractual Jurisdiction**:
- Before departure, colonists choose legal framework
- Compact specifies courts and rules
- Liability applies within the compact
- Multiple compacts can compete

---

## Governance of Colonies

### The Liability State Approach

Colonies are **startup societies**:
- Founders write charter
- Investors buy in with full information
- Internal disputes resolved by colony courts
- External disputes (between colonies) resolved by Earth-based arbitration

### Sovereignty Questions

| Question | Proposed Answer |
|----------|-----------------|
| When is a colony independent? | When it's self-sustaining |
| What happens to citizens? | Dual citizenship until choice |
| Can colonies refuse dividends? | Yes, but must provide alternative |

## The Letters of Marque Redux

### Space Privateering

In the event of conflict:
- Nations issue "Letters of Marque" to space operators
- Authorized to capture enemy assets
- Legal framework for space warfare
- Economic incentive for defense participation

### Resource Competition

| Scenario | Resolution |
|----------|------------|
| Two companies claim same asteroid | First-in-time principle |
| Orbital slot dispute | Auction through National Trust |
| Debris liability | Strict liability on creator |

## Long-Term Vision

### Multi-Planetary Civilization

| Timeframe | Development |
|-----------|-------------|
| 2030-2050 | Artemis, commercial stations, first Mars attempts |
| 2050-2080 | Permanent lunar presence, Mars colonization |
| 2080-2120 | Asteroid mining, orbital habitats |
| 2120+ | Self-sustaining off-Earth population |

### The Ultimate Backstop

Multiple independent civilizations = species survival:
- No single point of failure
- Different governance experiments
- Competition and cooperation
