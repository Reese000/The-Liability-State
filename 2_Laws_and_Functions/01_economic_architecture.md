## The Georgist Engine

### Revenue Sources

The state collects revenue from **three** sources only—no income tax, no corporate tax, no VAT:

| Source | Mechanism | Purpose |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Land Value Tax (LVT)** | Budget-First Cap on land rental value | Primary revenue |
| **Pollution/Extraction Fees** | Costs for environmental impact | Internalize externalities |
| **Liability Settlements** | Insurance payouts for damages | Compensation (not state revenue) |

### Why Land Value Tax?

**Economic Theory**: LVT is the "perfect tax":
- Land supply is perfectly inelastic (taxing it doesn't reduce supply)
- Unlike income/capital taxes, it doesn't discourage work or investment
- Captures "unearned increment" (value created by community, not landowner)
- Eliminates deadweight loss

**Estimated Impact**: Studies suggest replacing current taxes with LVT could increase output by ~26% through removal of friction on labor and capital.

## The Citizen's Dividend

### How It Works

1. LVT + Pollution Fees + Fines flow into **National Trust**
2. Essential state costs deducted (defense auditing, courts, minimal administration)
3. **Surplus distributed equally** to all citizens

### Key Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Universal** | Everyone receives it, regardless of employment |
| **Subsistence Floor** | A fixed national amount (e.g., $10K/year), calculated as bare subsistence in the cheapest habitable area of the entire system |
| **Non-Distorting** | No welfare cliff; working always adds to income |
| **Geographic Arbitrage** | The amount is the same everywhere. Non-workers must move to low-cost rural areas, organically dispersing the population across underutilized land |

### Why a Fixed National Floor (Not Variable Per County)

The dividend is a single national number, recalculated periodically against subsistence costs in the cheapest area. It does not vary by county or compact. This design was chosen over a variable-county model for the following reasons:

| Fixed National Floor | Variable (Per County) |
|---|---|
| Non-workers spread out across all cheap areas → rural repopulation | Non-workers concentrate in bottom-10% of counties → ghettos |
| Simple to administer — one number for the entire system | Complex calculation per county, open to data manipulation |
| No perverse local incentives | Counties could compete to keep costs low to avoid attracting people |
| Creates organic migration to underpopulated areas | People cluster in specific counties only |
| The cheapest area shifts over time as people move in and raise costs — no county stays cheapest for long | Static targeting creates permanent "welfare counties" |

### The Self-Adjusting Target

The floor is recalculated periodically (e.g., every 5 years) against the current cheapest habitable area. As people move to the cheapest county, rents there rise, and the cheapest county shifts to the next one. This creates a **moving target** — no single area bears the concentration indefinitely, and the population naturally disperses across the landscape.

### Inter-Compact Floor Considerations

The floor is set at the **national (Alliance) level** — a constitutional minimum that applies in every compact. Compacts may **supplement** the national floor with their own funds (additional dividend paid from their own LVT surplus), but they may not reduce it. This prevents a race-to-the-bottom between compacts while preserving the dispersal mechanism.

| Model | Effect | Perverse Incentive |
|---|---|---|
| National fixed floor | Same $10K everywhere. Non-workers move to cheap compacts | None — the floor is constitutional and applies system-wide |
| Compact-determined floors | Compact A sets $15K, Compact B sets $5K | Race to the bottom — compacts compete to lower their floor and repel welfare recipients |
| National floor + compact supplement | Floor is ≥ $10K. Rich compacts can add more | Healthy competition — compacts compete on generosity, without a floor to fall through |

### The "Baby Factory" Prevention

**Problem**: If children receive dividend, parents might have children for the money.

**Solution**: The Blind Trust Lockbox
- Children receive full dividend from birth
- **0%** goes to parents
- Money locked in trust, accumulating interest
- Unlocks at age 18 (or trade school completion)
- Parents face all costs (diapers, food) with no profit

**Result**: Every 18-year-old enters workforce with significant capital stake.

## Corporate Structure

### Graduated Liability (Not Instant Ruin)

In the Liability State, **liability is priced, not punitive**:

| Scenario | Consequence |
|----------|-------------|
| Routine accident | Insurance pays victim; premiums rise |
| Repeated negligence | Premiums become unaffordable; market forces exit |
| Calculated negligence | Veil pierced; executives lose personal assets |
| Operating uninsured | Illegal; automatic license revocation |
| Hiding operations | **Glass Box Violation**; loss of all legal shield |

### The Glass Box Mandate

To maintain the "Privilege of Commerce," all corporations must operate as **Glass Boxes**:
- **Real-Time Logbook**: All safety audits, emission levels, and liability-linked financial reserves are public.
- **Auditable Actuarial Models**: Any internal risk model used to negotiate insurance must be auditable by any citizen affected by the company's "Risk Footprint."
- **Asset Visibility**: Concealing ownership or assets to evade potential liability judgments is a criminal act of "Strategic Sabotage."

### Why This Matters

**Moral Hazard Eliminated**: When insurers pay for mistakes, they become obsessive inspectors. Companies don't face "ruin"—they face premium consequences proportional to their risk profile.

**Behavioral Shift**: Historical data from unlimited liability banking (California 1849-1930s) shows:
- Higher capital ratios
- Less speculative lending
- Extreme robustness against failures
- Economy dominated by prudent partnerships

### Natural Decentralization

Without limited liability protection, large companies **voluntarily split**:
- Each subsidiary quarantines liability
- One factory explosion doesn't bankrupt the global entity
- Creates a "lean" ecosystem of smaller, efficient firms
- Prevents "too big to fail" situations

## Anti-Monopoly Physics

### The Insurance Ceiling

As companies grow, their potential liability scales non-linearly:

1. **Small Company** → Small potential lawsuit → Low insurance cost
2. **Large Company** → Massive potential lawsuit → High insurance cost
3. **Monopoly** → Catastrophic potential lawsuit → **Uninsurable**

**The Mechanism**: Insurance markets have finite capacity. If potential loss exceeds sector solvency, coverage is impossible.

**Result**: Companies hit a natural "Too Big to Insure" ceiling and must break up into smaller, insurable units.

### Systemic Risk Multiplier

Courts can apply **scaled damages** based on market share:
- 5% market share company: Pays cleanup costs
- 80% market share company: Pays cleanup + "Systemic Risk Multiplier"

This makes monopoly economically irrational.

## Revenue Sufficiency Analysis

### The Fiscal Gap Challenge

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| US Total Land Value | ~$24 Trillion |
| Rental Value (5% cap rate) | ~$1.2 Trillion/year |
| Current US Federal Spending | ~$6.13 Trillion |
| **Gap** | ~$4.9 Trillion |

### Solutions to Close the Gap

1. **Drastic State Reduction**: The Liability State cannot just swap taxes—it must dismantle most current spending. Dividend replaces all distinct welfare programs.

2. **Expand "Land" Definition**: Include all monopoly rents:
   - Electromagnetic spectrum
   - Orbital slots
   - Intellectual property terms
   - Pollution fees (become critical, not supplementary)

3. **Double Dividend Effect**: Eliminating income tax spurs growth → raises land values → increases tax base (dynamic feedback loop).
