## The Incentive Tax Suite

Taxation in the Liability State isn't about funding bureaucracy—it's about **steering behavior**. Taxes punish unwanted actions and reward beneficial ones.

---

## The Cobra Effect Warning

Before implementing any incentive tax, understand the failure mode:

**The Parable**: British government in India wanted fewer cobras. Offered bounty for dead cobras. Locals started **breeding** cobras to kill for money. When bounty canceled, breeders released snakes. **More cobras than before**.

### Historical Failures

| Policy | Intended Effect | Actual Result |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| Cash for Clunkers | Help environment | Destroyed cheap used cars, hurt poor |
| Yacht Tax (1990) | Tax the rich | Rich bought abroad; US boat industry collapsed |
| Ethanol subsidies | Clean energy | Corn prices spiked; rainforest cleared |

**Lesson**: Every incentive creates unintended consequences.

## Primary Revenue: Land Value Tax (LVT)

### The Budget-First Cap

The LVT rate is **not** 100% extraction. It is calculated as:

```
LVT Rate = (Defense + Courts + Subsistence Floor) / Total Land Value
```

This ensures:
- Government only collects what is strictly necessary
- If government shrinks, taxes drop automatically
- No incentive to pump land values for bigger treasury
- Rate is **constitutionally locked** (75% supermajority to change)
- **Zero-Sum Budget**: Every increase in state spending results in a mathematically certain, immediate reduction in the Citizen Dividend.

### Why LVT Is Efficient

| Feature | Benefit |
|---------|---------|
| Land supply is inelastic | Cannot be "produced" to avoid tax |
| Doesn't discourage work | Unlike income tax |
| Captures "unearned increment" | Value from community, not landowner effort |
| Zero deadweight loss | Perfect tax mathematically |

### Harberger Tax: Self-Assessment Without Bureaucracy

**The Problem**: Traditional LVT requires armies of government assessors—creating bureaucracy.

**The Solution**: Owners assess their own property value.

| Rule | Effect |
|------|--------|
| You declare your property's value | You pay tax based on that number |
| Anyone can buy at your declared price | Forces honest valuation |

### The Option Premium Model (Anti-Griefing)

**The Old Problem**: If someone could instantly buy at your price, competitors could "grief" you by forcing sales on sentimental property.

**The Fix**: Buyers don't get instant purchase — there's a **two-phase process** with a free negotiation window.

| Phase | Duration | Buyer's Position | Owner's Position |
|-------|----------|------------------|------------------|
| **Free Window** | 14 days from bond post | Capital locked in escrow. Bond returned immediately if refused. | Can refuse with **no cash penalty**. Tax basis increases to offer price. |
| **Penalty Phase** | Day 15 → 90 days | Capital remains locked. | Can refuse by paying **15%** of offer price to buyer. |

#### Step-by-Step

| Step | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| 1. **Bid** | Buyer posts a **90-day bond** at the declared price (capital tied up for 90 days after the free window ends — 104 days total max). |
| 2. **Free Refusal (Days 1-14)** | Owner can refuse with zero cash penalty. The owner's **tax basis** increases to the offered price, raising their annual tax bill. |
| 3. **Penalty Refusal (Days 15-90)** | Owner can refuse by paying the buyer **15%** of the offer price. |
| 4. **Acceptance** | Owner accepts → sale proceeds at declared price. Bond returns to buyer. |

#### Why These Numbers

| Parameter | Value | Why |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| **Free window** | 14 days | Gives owner two weekends to negotiate, get advice, or arrange a counter-offer. Buyer loses ~$2K on $1M at 5% — acceptable friction. |
| **Penalty** | 15% of offer | ~3 years of typical asset yield (at 5% cap rate). Painful but doesn't force a fire sale. Owner could get a loan against the increased property value. |
| **Bond lockup** | 90 days maximum | Forces serious buyers without tying up capital for half a year. |

#### Benefits

- **Discourages griefing**: Bidder ties up capital for up to 104 days. Even if the owner refuses in the free window, the bidder's capital was locked — real cost for a frivolous offer.
- **Discourages low-balling**: Penalty is immediate cash loss, not future tax.
- **No bureaucrats**: Market forces honest valuation.
- **Liquidity shock protection**: The free window prevents the "you owe $200K out of nowhere" problem. Owners have 14 days to refuse for free.
- **The Pride Tax**: Owners who refuse during the penalty phase pay a voluntary premium above market usage value to satisfy their sentimental attachment, funding the community's general dividend.

### Why the Option Premium Is Necessary (Not Redundant)

**Question**: If a buyer must commit capital up front to make an offer, doesn't that already prevent griefing?

**Answer**: No. The Option Premium solves three problems that capital commitment alone does not:

| Problem | Capital-Only | With Option Premium |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Griefing by tying up assets** | Rival ties up $10M for 104 days — costs them ~1.4% in lost returns. Cheap harassment. | Rival risks 15% penalty ($1.5M) just to attempt a forced sale. Not cheap. |
| **&quot;I changed my mind&quot;** | Owner forced to sell if market conditions change. No flexibility. | Owner has 14 free days to decide. After that, 15% penalty preserves adaptability. |
| **Pride Tax revenue** | Owners under-declare to avoid forced sale → lower tax base. | Owners can declare true value and use the free window + penalty as insurance against forced sale. Revenue funds the Dividend. |

**Conclusion**: Capital commitment filters out *poor* griefers. The Option Premium filters out *wealthy and motivated* ones. The 14-day free window prevents liquidity shocks. Both are needed.

### Revenue Futures (Fiscal Stability)

**The Problem**: LVT revenue crashes during recessions (land values drop), but government costs are fixed.

**The Old Solution**: Government reserves (prone to political raiding).

**The Market Solution**: Sell **LVT Futures** to global investors.

| Phase | Mechanism |
|-------|-----------|
| **Issuance** | Treasury sells rights to next year's LVT revenue |
| **Boom** | Investors pay premium; state gets stable cash |
| **Bust** | Investors absorb the loss; state cashflow unchanged |

**Why This Works**: Global capital markets have higher risk tolerance than municipal governments. The state doesn't hold reserves—it offloads volatility risk to Wall Street.

### What Counts as "Land"

Expanded definition includes all monopoly rents:
- Physical land
- Electromagnetic spectrum (Auto-taxed by claim)
- Orbital slots (Mercantile Registry)
- Intellectual property terms
- Extraction rights

**No-Owner Homesteading**: Resources are not "given" by the state. They are "Homesteaded" by anyone willing to declare a Harberger Price and pay the 3% LVT. The state acts only as a **Notary of Exclusive Use**.

## The Feebate Model (Revenue Neutrality)

### The Safe Way to Incentivize

A Feebate is a **closed-loop system**—government spends nothing. Money shifts from "bad actors" to "good actors" within the same sector.

### Example: Vehicles

| Action | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Buy gas guzzler (15 MPG) | Pay $2,000 fee at dealership |
| Buy efficient car (45 MPG) | Receive $2,000 rebate |

**The Fee Funds the Rebate**—no treasury drain.

### Why Feebates Work

- **Self-Funding**: Costs state $0
- **Dynamic**: If everyone buys efficient cars, fees dry up—but so do rebates
- **Self-Terminating**: System turns off when goal achieved

## Inheritance Tax

### The Merit Razor

Large inheritance tax aligns with meritocratic ideal—no "trust fund babies."

| Goal | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| Reset scoreboard each generation | High inheritance tax (50%+) |
| Circulate capital | Force wealth back into economy |
| Prevent aristocracy | Break dynastic accumulation |

### The Liquidity Problem

**Scenario**: You own a $10M business but only $100k in cash. You die. Government sends heir a $5M bill due in 9 months.

**Result**: Heir must sell business (often at fire-sale price) just to pay tax.

### The Fix: Equity Payment

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Heir can't pay in cash | Pay in non-voting equity |
| Forces sale of productive businesses | Government/Trust takes 50% of shares |
| Destroys family enterprises | Heir keeps control, runs business |
| | 50% of future dividends go to public |

**Result**: Business survives; "unearned wealth" flows to citizens.

## Sin Taxes (Actuarial, Not Moral)

### The Liability Approach

Don't tax cigarettes to fund general government. Tax them to **fund Cancer Treatment Risk Pool**.

| If You Smoke | Your Surcharge Goes To |
|--------------|------------------------|
| Pay extra fee | High-Risk Health Insurance pool |
| Pre-paying liability | Your future medical costs |

**Not A Moral Tax**—an actuarial one.

## Vacancy Tax

### The Ghost City Killer

**Problem**: Rich investors buy apartments in cities and leave them empty as "safe deposit boxes."

**Effect**: Drives up rents for actual workers.

### The Fix

| Condition | Tax Rate |
|-----------|----------|
| Property occupied | Normal LVT |
| Unoccupied 6+ months/year | **Triple** LVT rate |

**Incentive**: Rent it out (increasing supply) or sell it.

## Tobin Tax (Financial Transaction)

### Speeding Ticket for Finance

**Problem**: High-Frequency Trading algorithms skim pennies with millisecond trades. Zero economic value, destabilizes markets.

### The Fix

Tiny tax (0.1%) on every financial transaction.

| Investor Type | Impact |
|---------------|--------|
| Long-term investor (10-year hold) | Negligible |
| Algorithm (1,000 trades/second) | Profit margin destroyed |

**Effect**: Pushes market back toward investing, not gambling.

## Severance Tax

### The Raw Material Tax

**Problem**: Nature (oil, timber, water) treated as "free" until depleted.

### The Fix

Tax resources at **Point of Extraction**.

| Effect | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| New materials become expensive | Recycling becomes profitable |
| Circular economy emerges | Scrap is tax-free |
| Conservation incentivized | No need to ban anything |

## The Automation Ratio Shift

### The Robot Tax Alternative

Don't tax the robot (discourages innovation). Tax the **ratio**.

| Human-to-Revenue Ratio | LVT Rate |
|-----------------------|----------|
| High (employing people) | Discount |
| Low (fully automated) | Full rate |

**Effect**: Subtle financial pressure to keep humans in the loop when marginally useful.

## Full Expensing (Aggressive Investment)

### The CapEx Incentive

| Profit | Spending | Taxable Income |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| $1M | $1M on new equipment | $0 |
| $1M | $0 (kept as cash) | $1M (taxed) |

**Effect**: Forces companies to spend profits on equipment, R&D, expansion. Makes "hoarding cash" expensive. Drives high-velocity economy.
