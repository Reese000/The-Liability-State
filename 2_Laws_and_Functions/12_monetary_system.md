## The Currency Problem

Every monetary system must solve three problems simultaneously:

| Property | What It Means | Why It's Hard |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| **Stable store of value** | Savings don't evaporate | Requires supply discipline |
| **Elastic supply** | Money supply grows with the economy | Requires discretion or automatic mechanism |
| **No central issuer** | No one can print money for political ends | Requires commodity or algorithm |

Most systems pick two and struggle with the third:

| System | Stable store | Elastic supply | No central issuer |
|--------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **Gold standard** | ✅ | ❌ (inelastic — deflationary) | ✅ |
| **Fiat (Central Bank)** | ❌ (lost 95%+ of value in every case) | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Bitcoin** | ⚠️ (volatile) | ❌ (fixed schedule) | ✅ |
| **Land-backed (LS)** | ✅ | ✅ (tracks real economy) | ✅ |

### Why Gold Won't Work

| Problem | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Supply shocks** | Asteroid mining, deep-sea nodules, or a new extraction technology could double gold supply overnight, halving the purchasing power of gold-backed currency. A massive wealth transfer from savers to miners. |
| **Bankers create private fiat on top** | The gold standard never existed as a pure gold medium of exchange — it was always gold-*backed* fiat. Banks issued notes redeemable in gold, then lent at fractional reserves. The gold was the reserve, not the medium. Bank runs still happened. |
| **Stockpiling waste** | Gold used as money is gold not used in electronics, jewelry, or industry. The stock-to-flow ratio of monetary gold is ~60:1 — 60 years' worth of mining production locked in vaults doing nothing productive. |

### Why Fiat Won't Work

| Problem | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Central bank capture** | Every major fiat currency has lost 95%+ of its value eventually. The question isn't "what inflation rate is optimal" — it's "can you commit to the rule?" History says no. |
| **Political discretion** | A central bank with the power to set interest rates and print money is the single most capturable institution in any system. The Liability State has no mechanism to prevent it. |
| **System incompatibility** | The LS has no central bank and no policy rate. Standard arguments for managed inflation (room to cut rates, wage stickiness) don't apply because the system doesn't have those features. |

### Why Land

Land has three unique properties no other asset possesses:

| Property | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| **Perfectly inelastic supply** | Land cannot be mined, manufactured, or discovered. No supply shock risk. No asteroid mining threat. |
| **Already assessed** | The LVT system generates land valuations for every parcel. No additional infrastructure needed. |
| **LVT prevents hoarding** | You cannot speculate in the backing asset — the LVT captures the rental value, making unproductive land holding uneconomic. Contrast with gold, where hoarding is the natural strategy. |

## The Definitional Peg Model

The currency is **not** convertible into land (land is immobile). Instead, it operates on a **definitional peg**:

> **One LS Dollar is constitutionally defined as 1/1,000,000th of the Land Value Index (LVI) — the total assessed value of all LVT-taxable assets divided by the number of currency units in circulation.**

### How the Peg Is Enforced (No Central Bank)

The National Trust does not actively issue or redeem currency against the index. The peg is enforced through **fiscal policy**:

| Force | Direction | Mechanism |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **Demand anchor** | People need the currency | LVT is collected in LS Dollars. You must acquire them to pay your tax obligation. |
| **Supply anchor** | People receive the currency | The Citizen's Dividend is paid in LS Dollars. Citizens receive them as income. |
| **Stability** | Natural equilibrium | The state's primary revenue source and primary expenditure are both in the same unit. This creates a closed loop that stabilizes the currency's purchasing power without any central bank action. |

### Why This Works Without a Central Bank

The Liability State has no central bank because it doesn't need one:

| Conventional central bank function | Why the LS doesn't need it |
|-----------------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Monetary policy** (set interest rates) | No policy rate. Capital allocation is market-determined through bonds, royalties, and partnerships. |
| **Lender of last resort** | Private insurance and mutualist finance provide liquidity. The War Escrow and private bond markets handle crisis funding. |
| **Currency issuance** | The currency supply is determined by the Land Value Index mechanically. No discretion. |
| **Inflation targeting** | The peg to the LVI is the inflation target. If land values rise 3% in a year, the currency's real value rises ~3% (mild deflation). If land values fall, the currency depreciates (mild inflation). |

## The Land Value Index (LVI)

### Composition

The LVI comprises all assets subject to Harberger self-assessment and LVT. The full basket includes:

| Component | Examples | Weight Floor |
|-----------|----------|:-----------:|
| **Physical land** | Residential, commercial, agricultural, undeveloped | ≥60% |
| **Electromagnetic spectrum** | Frequency bands, broadcast licenses, satellite slots | Variable |
| **Orbital slots** | Geostationary positions, orbital paths | Variable |
| **Intellectual property** | Harberger-patented inventions, copyright terms | Variable |
| **Extraction rights** | Mineral, oil, gas, water extraction permits | Variable |

**Physical land must constitute at least 60% of the LVI by weight.** This is a constitutional floor — it prevents volatility in IP, spectrum, or extraction markets from destabilizing the currency. The remaining 40% captures the broader economy and diversifies the backing.

### How It's Calculated

```
LVI = Sum(LVT-assessed value of all assets) / Currency units in circulation
```

The LVT assessment methodology generates valuations for each asset using the Harberger self-assessment model (see [Taxation §The Option Premium Model](./10_taxation.md#harberger-tax-self-assessment-without-bureaucracy)). These valuations are:

- **Public** — any citizen can verify them
- **Contestable** — a citizen who believes an asset is undervalued can buy it at the declared price
- **Aggregated** — the National Trust publishes the index daily

### What Prevents Manipulation

| Attack | Defense |
|--------|---------|
| **Under-assess land to shrink the LVI** | Harberger self-assessment forces honest valuation — anyone can buy at your declared price |
| **Over-assess land to inflate the LVI** | You pay LVT based on your declared value, so over-assessment is self-punishing |
| **Manipulate the basket composition** | The 60% physical land floor is constitutional. The weight of each component is calculated from trailing 5-year average LVT revenue, not a point-in-time snapshot. |
| **Collude to move a large parcel's value** | The index is a broad basket across all compacts. Moving it requires coordinated manipulation of thousands of parcels — which the LVT system's transparency requirements make impossible. |

## Currency Supply Dynamics

### Growth Phase (Expanding Economy)

1. Population grows → demand for land increases
2. Land values rise → LVT revenue increases
3. LVI grows (numerator increases)
4. Currency supply in circulation is unchanged
5. **Each unit of currency represents a claim on a larger asset base → mild deflation (~1-3%/year in high-growth periods)**

### Contraction Phase (Recession)

1. Economic activity slows → land values fall
2. LVT revenue declines
3. LVI shrinks (numerator decreases)
4. Currency supply in circulation is unchanged
5. **Each unit of currency represents a claim on a smaller asset base → mild inflation (~1-3%/year in recessions)**

### The Citizen's Dividend as Natural Stabilizer

The Dividend puts purchasing power into citizens' hands. In a growth phase, the Dividend rises (more LVT revenue), offsetting the deflationary pressure of the appreciating currency. In a recession, the Dividend falls (less LVT revenue), which is mildly contractionary but prevents the system from over-stimulating.

## Territorial Expansion and the Currency

When the Liability State acquires new territory — whether through peaceful settlement, incorporation of a new compact, or the conquest framework described in [Defense §External Conquest Doctrine](./07_defense_military.md#external-conquest-doctrine) — the new land is assessed for LVT and added to the index.

### The Phase-In Rule

Newly acquired territory is phased into the LVI over **10 years**:

| Year | Fraction of assessed value included |
|:----:|:----------------------------------:|
| 1-2 | 0% (stabilization period) |
| 3 | 20% |
| 4 | 30% |
| 5 | 40% |
| 6 | 50% |
| 7 | 60% |
| 8 | 70% |
| 9 | 85% |
| 10 | 100% |

**Why**: This prevents sudden currency shocks from territorial changes. Whether the territory was acquired peacefully or through conquest, the currency base adjusts slowly. No overnight windfall for currency holders.

### Space Frontier

A new settlement on a lunar or off-world frontier begins with very low land value (no infrastructure, tiny population, minimal productivity). As the colony develops and land values rise:

1. The settlement is assessed for LVT using the same Harberger model
2. It is phased into the LVI over 10 years from the date of first assessment
3. The currency appreciates gradually as the frontier matures
4. The Dividend distributes the benefit of frontier growth to all citizens

This is the system working as designed: the currency reflects real economic development, not speculative claims on future territory.

## Fractional-Reserve Banking and Private Credit

The Liability State cannot ban credit creation. Private banks will exist and will lend against reserves at fractional ratios, creating private money as they do in any system.

### How the System Disincentivizes Speculative Credit

| Feature | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| **LVT makes land the most attractive reserve asset** | Banks prefer to hold land-backed instruments rather than speculative credit. Capital flows to productive real assets. |
| **Unlimited shareholder liability** | Bank executives are personally liable for their bank's actions. This makes fractional-reserve lending far more conservative than in the current system (where limited liability protects bankers from the consequences of bad loans). |
| **No lender of last resort** | There is no central bank to bail out failing banks. Banks that overextend fail. The insurance market prices this risk accurately. |
| **Glass Box Mandate** | All bank balance sheets are public. Depositors can see exactly how much the bank is lending against its reserves. A bank with a 20:1 ratio faces immediate withdrawal pressure. |

**Historical precedent**: Unlimited liability banking in California (1849-1930s) produced higher capital ratios, less speculative lending, extreme robustness against failures, and an economy dominated by prudent partnerships. The same dynamics apply here.

## Inflation, Deflation, and the Optimal Regime

| Regime | Incentive Created | Who It Helps | Who It Hurts |
|--------|-------------------|:------------:|:------------:|
| **Inflation (+2%/yr)** | Spend now, don't hold cash | Debtors, the state | Savers, wage earners, retirees |
| **Neutral (0%)** | No distortion | Long-term planners | Hard to maintain mechanically |
| **Deflation (-1-2%/yr)** | Save, take only good debt | Savers, creditors | Debtors (real burden rises) |
| **Land-pegged (LS)** | Tracks real growth | Everyone (systemic stability) | Slightly deflationary in booms, inflationary in busts |

The land-pegged currency is **slightly deflationary in high-growth periods** (land values rise slower than productivity in a tech boom) and **slightly inflationary in recessions** (land values fall slower than output). This is counter-cyclical — the currency tightens in booms and loosens in busts — without anyone deciding to do that. It is the only regime that achieves this automatically.

## Summary

| Feature | Design |
|---------|--------|
| **Type** | Definitional peg to the Land Value Index |
| **Backing** | All LVT-taxable assets (≥60% physical land) |
| **Issuer** | No central bank — fiscal anchor through LVT/Dividend loop |
| **Supply** | Demand-determined at the peg through land value growth |
| **Expansion** | New territory phased in over 10 years |
| **Private credit** | Allowed but disincentivized by unlimited liability and LVT |
| **Inflation/deflation** | Counter-cyclical by design — no policy rate |
