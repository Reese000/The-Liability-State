## The Core Thesis

The Liability State represents a **paradigmatic shift** in the social contract: replacing the administrative welfare state with a market-adjudicated order rooted in priced liability, Georgist economics, and decentralized governance.

> **The Principle**: Free action is permitted, but the actor's risk is accurately priced by the insurance market. "Permissionless Innovation" meets "Priced Responsibility."

---

## The Fundamental Transition

| Current System (Ex-Ante) | Liability State (Ex-Post) |
|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Government sets rules before you act | You act freely, but pay for damages after |
| Bureaucratic inspectors approve safety | Insurance companies verify safety (profit motive) |
| Fines for violations | Lawsuits for actual harm |
| Regulatory capture by lobbyists | Decentralized courts resist capture |
| Compliance theater | Genuine risk management |

## The Three Pillars

### 1. Greed (Profit/Dividend)
Citizens are shareholders in their nation. When the economy thrives, the Citizen's Dividend grows. Self-interest aligns with national interest.

### 2. Fear (Liability/Premium Pressure)
Bad actors face escalating insurance costs, not bureaucratic fines. Chronic negligence leads to unaffordable premiums, forcing market exit without government enforcement.

### 3. Pride (Guilds/Status)
In a post-scarcity world, reputation and mastery replace wealth as status markers. Guild membership and community standing drive behavior.
