## The Regency Period

### Concept

A 10-20 year transitional phase where the government **slowly retreats** as private sector proves capability.

### Why It's Necessary

| Immediate Switch | Gradual Transition |
|------------------|-------------------|
| Legal vacuum | Parallel systems |
| Economic chaos | Proven alternatives |
| Power grabs | Controlled handover |
| No case law | Precedent accumulates |

---

## Phase 1: Foundation (Years 1-5)

### Legal Infrastructure

| Action | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| Certify first Private Courts | Build alternative judiciary |
| Require case law publication | Create precedent database |
| Allow voluntary arbitration | Let parties test private courts |
| Reform tort law | Enable strict liability framework |

### Financial Infrastructure

| Action | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| Launch LVT pilot zones | Test revenue viability |
| Create liability insurance markets | Build capacity |
| Establish certification bodies | Train Trust Brokers |
| Reform corporate liability (executives first) | Begin piercing veil |

### Political Infrastructure

| Action | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| Constitutional convention | Lock core principles |
| Create House of Repeal (advisory first) | Train sortition process |
| Transparency laws | Open government books |
| Sunset clauses on new regulations | Stop entropy |

---

## Phase 2: Parallel Operation (Years 5-10)

### Courts

- Private courts handle **50% of civil cases**
- Government courts remain for criminal matters
- Litigants can choose forum
- Reputation data accumulates

### Revenue

- LVT covers **30% of municipal revenue**
- Income tax rates begin declining
- Dividend distributed in pilot regions
- Corporate liability reforms expand

### Regulation

- Major industries can opt into **insurance-as-regulator** model
- Traditional regulation remains as fallback
- Data collected on which approach works better
- Trust Broker ecosystem matures

---

## Phase 3: Handover (Years 10-15)

### Courts

- Private courts become **primary** civil judiciary
- Government courts handle only appeals and constitutional issues
- Certification standards proven
- "Loser Pays" becomes default

### Revenue

- LVT becomes **primary** revenue source
- Income tax eliminated for bottom 80%
- Full Citizen's Dividend begins
- Pollution/extraction fees mature

### Regulation

- Most sectors under **insurance regulation**
- Government inspectors become auditors
- Certification bodies ISO-standardized
- Market for trust fully liquid

---

## Phase 4: Consolidation (Years 15-20)

### Final State

- Government reduced to **core functions**:
  - Defense auditing
  - Court certification
  - National Trust management
  - Currency issuance
  
- All other functions **privatized or decentralized**
- Constitutional protections locked
- Regional Compacts operational
- Full Liability State achieved

## Risk Management During Transition

### The Looting Risk

**Historical Warning**: Post-Soviet Russia—elites privatized state assets into personal wealth.

**Mitigations**:
| Risk | Protection |
|------|------------|
| Elite capture | Transparent asset transfers |
| Privatization fraud | Competitive bidding required |
| Political blocking | Constitutional lock-in |
| Judicial corruption | Multiple private court options |

### The Intent Gap: Punishment vs. Liability

During the transition period, the old state's enforcement capacity is declining but the new courts are not yet fully operational. Actors may exploit this gap to extract assets. The system addresses this through a **principled distinction between how different types of transition-era conduct are treated after the new courts come online**:

| Type of Conduct | Treatment | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Intentional taking** (theft, fraud, expropriation, asset stripping) | **Retroactive punishment** by the new private courts. Full liability + punitive damages. | The old state's laws against theft, fraud, and conversion were in force at the time. The new courts enforce *existing* law that nobody was enforcing during the gap. |
| **Honest transaction** (a deal made in good faith that later turns out to be harmful) | **Compensatory liability only**. Make the victim whole. No punishment. | No intent to harm. The parties could not have known at the time. Restitution is sufficient. |
| **Negligent transaction** (reckless or grossly negligent dealings during the transition) | **Liability for full damages**. Premium consequences. Insurer subrogation. | The party should have known better but didn't actively intend harm. Standard liability applies. |

### The Transparency First Principle

The mechanism does not require active enforcement during the transition. It requires **architectural transparency**:

> "Conduct all transactions during the transition with the care you would if you knew every detail would be reviewed by a court in 5 years. Because it will."

The financial system is designed from day 1 such that no opaque transaction is possible (Glass Box design). Every asset movement, contract formation, and financial flow is recorded on a public ledger. Even with zero active enforcement during the collapse period, *every extraction leaves a trail*.

**How this deters looting**:
1. Before the transition: the system's constitution announces that all transition-era transactions are publicly recorded
2. During the transition: looters can take assets, but every move is visible
3. After the transition: private courts come online with the full record. Victims file liability claims. Looters face the full consequences

For rational actors, the calculus shifts from "free grab" to "high-risk theft with delayed but certain consequences." For irrational or ideologically-motivated actors, the transparent record enables asset recovery after the fact.

### The Public Advisory

During the transition, the following warning is published and distributed through all available channels:

> **"The Liability State transition is underway. All financial and contractual transactions are being publicly recorded. After the transition, private courts will review the transition record. If you take what is not yours, you will face retroactive liability. If you transact in good faith and the deal turns out harmful, you will be made whole — no punishment. Conduct yourselves accordingly."**

This advisory itself serves as notice: any party who proceeds with asset extraction after receiving this warning cannot later claim they did not know the rules.

### The Chaos Risk

**Concern**: Gap between old rules and new rules enables harm.

**Mitigations**:
| Risk | Protection |
|------|------------|
| Regulatory gap | Parallel systems during transition |
| Uninsured actors | Mandatory coverage phase-in |
| Case law vacuum | Grandfathered precedents |
| Enforcement gap | Legacy enforcement during overlap |

### The Resistance Risk

**Concern**: Bureaucrats and regulated industries resist change.

**Mitigations**:
| Risk | Protection |
|------|------------|
| Bureaucratic obstruction | Sunset clauses force action |
| Industry lobbying | Competition from new entrants |
| Public fear | Pilot program success stories |
| Political reversal | Constitutional entrenchment |
