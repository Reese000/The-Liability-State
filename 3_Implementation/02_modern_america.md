## The Starting Point

### Current US Structure

```
Federal Government
    └── 50 States
        └── ~3,000 Counties
            └── ~20,000 Cities/Towns
```

### Target Structure

```
Federal Auditor (Defense, Courts, Currency)
    └── Regional Compacts (~50, replacing states)
        └── Municipal Corporations (~20,000)
            └── Individual Property Owners
```

## Federal Level Transformation

### What Remains

| Function | New Role |
|----------|----------|
| **Defense** | Audits PMC bonds, manages escrowed munitions |
| **National Trust** | Manages LVT revenue, distributes Dividend |
| **Supreme Court** | Final appeals, constitutional interpretation |
| **Currency** | Maintains dollar (or successor) |
| **Strategist** | Proposes National Missions |
| **House of Repeal** | Removes laws, fires auditors |

### What Dissolves

| Current Agency | Transition |
|----------------|------------|
| **IRS** | Becomes LVT collection (simpler) |
| **EPA** | Becomes pollution fee collector |
| **FDA** | Shifts to insurance/certification |
| **OSHA** | Shifts to liability insurers |
| **DOE** | Bounty system for energy goals |
| **HUD** | Municipal responsibility |
| **Most cabinet departments** | Functions privatized |

## State Level Transformation

### From States to Compacts

| Current State | New Compact |
|---------------|-------------|
| Legislature passes laws | Maintains legal "flavor" |
| Governor executes | Cultural custodian |
| State agencies regulate | Invoices cities for regional projects |
| State taxes citizens | No direct taxation |

### Georgia Compact Example

| Function | Implementation |
|----------|---------------|
| **Legal Framework** | "Georgia Common Law" defaults for courts |
| **Regional Projects** | I-75 maintenance, Chattahoochee Trust |
| **State Guard** | Volunteer militia for disaster relief |
| **Cultural Identity** | State fair, monuments, regional sports leagues |
| **Circuit Breaker** | Can nullify federal overreach |

### Revenue Model

- Compact invoices member cities (e.g., "Tucker, your share of Regional Rail is $5M")
- No income tax at state level
- If Compact becomes bloated, cities can dissolve and reform it

## Local Level Transformation

### Municipal Corporations

Cities become **subscription-based service providers**:

| Service | Funding |
|---------|---------|
| Roads | User fees, tolls |
| Trash | Service subscription |
| Parks | Commons Trust |
| Police | Private security + liability |
| Fire | Insurance-funded response |
| Schools | Education vouchers (parental choice) |

### Property Rights Focus

- Zoning handled by deed covenants, not regulations
- Noise/pollution handled by liability, not ordinance
- Building codes → insurance requirements

## Key Constitutional Amendments Needed

### Amendment 28: The Liability Clause

> No person or entity shall be shielded from liability for harm caused to another's person or property by corporate structure, employment status, or government mandate.

### Amendment 29: The Land Value Clause

> Congress shall levy no tax on income, labor, or capital gains. Revenue shall be derived from the rental value of land, natural resources, and pollution.

### Amendment 30: The Negative Legislature

> The House of Representatives shall be reconstituted as the House of Repeal. Members shall serve single terms, selected by lottery. The House may repeal existing laws by simple majority but may not enact new legislation.

### Amendment 31: The Transparency Clause

> All government expenditures shall be published in real-time. All classified documents shall be automatically declassified after ten years unless renewed by 75% vote of the House of Repeal.

### Amendment 32: The Dividend Clause

> Every citizen shall receive an equal share of surplus government revenue. This Dividend may not be reduced except by 75% vote of the House of Repeal.

## Political Pathway

### Step 1: State-Level Experimentation

- One or more states adopt LVT locally
- Charter cities test private court systems
- States opt into insurance-regulation pilots

### Step 2: Federal Enabling

- Constitutional convention called
- Amendments proposed and ratified
- Transition timeline established

### Step 3: Parallel Operation

- Federal and legacy state systems run alongside new system
- Citizens can opt into Liability State provisions
- Data collected on outcomes

### Step 4: Consolidation

- Legacy systems sunset
- Full transition achieved
- Constitutional protections locked
