# Class Warfare (Unequal Societies)

[← Back to Overview](./00_overview.md) | [Index](../README.md)

---

## The Risk: Redistributive Collapse

In societies with extreme inequality, the landless majority has both the numbers and the incentive to vote for confiscatory LVT rates, liquidating the propertied class to fund a massive temporary payout.

---

## The Mechanism

### 1. The 51% Coalition

Under the 75% supermajority lock, a bare majority cannot change the LVT rate. But what if the landless are **more than 75%** of the population?

| Population | Can change LVT? | Outcome |
|------------|-----------------|---------|
| 50% landless | No (needs 75%) | Lock holds |
| 75% landless | Yes | Lock fails; redistribution is constitutional |
| 90% landless | Yes | Certain |

In countries like Brazil, where the Gini coefficient is extreme, the landless class can exceed the supermajority threshold. The constitutional lock is only as strong as the demographic it protects.

### 2. The Sunk Cost Trap

A corporation sinks $10B into a mega-factory (immobile infrastructure). Workers move in. Once the factory is "trapped," the workers vote to raise LVT from 3% to 20% to milk the industry. The capital cannot leave without abandoning the investment.

### 3. Single-Compact Dependency

If the entire country is one Regional Compact, capital has **no exit option**. The Exodus Equilibrium (capital flees to a neighboring compact with lower taxes) requires neighboring compacts to exist. In a single-compact system, the market discipline on redistribution vanishes.

---

## Why Existing Mitigations Are Insufficient

| Mitigation | Why It Fails |
|------------|--------------|
| **75% supermajority lock** | Fails when landless >75% of population |
| **Harberger liquidation** | Owner can set price to $1 and walk away, but this destroys the tax base — no one wins |
| **Exodus Equilibrium** | Requires multiple competing compacts. If the whole country is one compact, capital has nowhere to go |
| **Stability Charters** | Insurers can void them, but if the compact refuses to comply, enforcement is unclear |
| **Dividend-as-Asset** | Dividend is already small in a poor country — the marginally higher payout from redistribution is more attractive |

---

## Mitigation Strategies

### Mitigation 1: Multi-Compact Mandate

The Liability State should **never be implemented as a single national compact**. The constitution must require a minimum of three competing Regional Compacts with independent tax-setting power. This ensures the Exodus Equilibrium is real.

### Mitigation 2: Pre-Transition Land Reform

Before the Liability State goes live, a one-time land redistribution places a minimum property stake in every citizen's hands. This shrinks the landless class below 75%. The reform happens *before* the constitutional locks are set, so it cannot be reversed.

### Mitigation 3: Escrowed Stability Bonds

During transition, landholders and citizens post Stability Bonds into an escrow held by the National Alliance. If the LVT rate exceeds the constitutional cap within the first 30 years, the bonds are forfeited to the opposite class. This aligns both classes against rate changes during the vulnerable period.

---

## Organic Resolution

The Class Warfare problem has no fully organic resolution in a single-compact system. The **Multi-Compact** structure is the primary mechanism — requiring competing Regional Compacts so the **Exodus Equilibrium** (capital fleeing high-tax zones) is real and immediate. In a single-compact state with extreme inequality, the Liability State will collapse into a redistributive spiral within one generation. The honest answer: **Pre-transition land reform is a prerequisite**, not an optional mitigation.

The honest answer: **The Liability State is designed for societies with moderate inequality and strong institutions.** In societies where >75% of the population is landless, the preconditions for the system do not exist, and a different reform path (land reform first, then Liability State) is required.

---

## Bottom Line

| Risk | Mitigation | Residual Risk |
|------|------------|---------------|
| Redistributive vote | Multi-Compact Mandate | Low (if enforced) |
| Sunk Cost Trap | Pre-transition land reform | Medium |
| Single-compact collapse | Escrowed Stability Bonds | High (the system is structurally weak here) |

**The honest assessment**: The Liability State is not a universal governance model. It works in societies with moderate inequality, functioning institutions, and enough geographic/cultural diversity to sustain multiple compacts. Applying it to a Brazil or South Africa without pre-reform is likely to trigger the very class warfare it was designed to prevent.
