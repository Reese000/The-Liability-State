## The Risk: Inter-Compact Friction and Resource Wars

Regional Compacts provide cultural "flavor" and local OS, but they are also competing entities. In a world of PMCs and market-driven defense, disputes between Compacts can escalate into systemic instability.

**However, the default mechanism for resolving inter-compact disputes is through the ordinary private court system.** The Supreme Trust Oracle is not the primary resolver — it is the **backstop enforcement layer** that activates only when private courts cannot complete the job.

---

## The Mechanism

The core friction mechanism is the same across all inter-compact conflict scenarios: **a shared resource or liability boundary crosses a governance boundary**. Neither compact's court system has authority over the other, and no compact can unilaterally enforce a solution. The mechanism plays out in three distinct patterns — resource conflicts, arbitrage wars, and bounty wars — all of which the private court system resolves by default, with the Supreme Trust Oracle as backstop.

---

## Default Resolution: Private Courts

### How Inter-Compact Disputes Go Through Private Courts

When Compact A and Compact B have a dispute — over river pollution, cross-border liability judgments, or regulatory arbitrage — they take it to any private court they both agree on (or a court chosen by the Actuarial Commons if they cannot agree). The court:

1. Hears the case under the law of the jurisdiction most relevant to the harm
2. Does not need to choose between conflicting compact laws — it looks for a **fair resolution within the context of the situation**
3. Issues a binding ruling

If the losing party is a **private actor** (a corporation, an individual, a guild), the court's enforcement is standard: attach assets, seize bond, garnish insurance. This covers 95%+ of inter-compact disputes.

### Why This Works

The key insight: **courts resolve conflicts of law through context, not compliance.** If Compact A's law says chemical dumping is allowed and Compact B says it's not, a private court does not need to decide which law is "correct." It asks: was there harm? Who caused it? What is fair compensation? The legal frameworks of the two compacts inform the court's reasoning, but the court is not bound to enforce either compact's interpretation against the other compact as a sovereign body.

## When Private Courts Fail: The Backstop Cases

Three scenarios exist where private courts cannot resolve the dispute, and the Supreme Trust Oracle must intervene:

### Scenario 1: The Compact-as-Defendant Problem

| Normal case | When the defendant is a compact |
|---|---|
| Court issues ruling against a corporation | Court orders seizure of corporate assets |
| Enforcement is automatic | Compact's assets (land, tax revenue, territory) are sovereign |
| Bond holder pays | Compact refuses to comply, has no bond in dispute |

If a compact itself is the defendant — not a company within it, but the compact government — and it refuses to comply with a private court ruling, a private court has no enforcement mechanism against a territorial governance entity. This is where the STO steps in.

### Scenario 2: The War of Judgments

| Private Court A (Compact A's choice) | Private Court B (Compact B's choice) |
|---|---|
| Rules in favor of Compact A | Rules in favor of Compact B |
| Orders damages from Compact B | Orders damages from Compact A |
| Both rulings are "valid" | No authority above both to break the tie |

If compact A refuses to participate in a jointly-chosen court and instead gets a ruling from its home court, and compact B does the same, you have two conflicting judgments with no authority above both. The STO breaks the tie.

### Scenario 3: The Multilateral Commons

A river flows through three compacts. Compact A dams it for irrigation. Compact B loses fisheries. Compact C gets less water downstream. This is not a bilateral dispute — it's a multilateral coordination problem with no single plaintiff and defendant. No private court has standing over all three compacts simultaneously. The STO enforces the inter-compact commons agreement (or creates one in the absence of agreement).

## The Supreme Trust Oracle

### Role

The STO is the **settlement layer** of the Liability State — the one body that coordinates the entire system because if it fails, the system does not hold together. It handles only the three backstop scenarios above, leaving all routine inter-compact disputes to the private market.

### Powers

| Power | What It Does | When Used |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust Blockade** | Invalidates Harberger land titles across ALL compacts. No compact may recognize the rogue compact's property titles. Land values in the rogue compact collapse to $0. | When a compact defiantly refuses to comply with multiple private court rulings |
| **Force Prohibition Enforcement** | Dissolves a compact that raises arms against another compact or against residents exercising constitutional rights | When a compact violates the constitutional ban on inter-compact military force |
| **Commons Agreement Enforcement** | Rules on breaches of inter-compact treaties for shared resources (rivers, fisheries, airsheds, spectrum) | When multilateral commons agreements are breached |
| **Good Faith Bond Liquidation** | Compacts escrow 10% of LVT revenue in an Alliance-held bond. STO rulings against a compact are paid from this escrow | When a compact loses an STO ruling — no confrontation, just liquidation |

### Why the Trust Blockade Is Unique

The Trust Blockade is the system's nuclear option, and no private court can perform it. It requires *every* compact to simultaneously refuse recognition of a single compact's land titles. Only the Alliance-level STO has standing to issue such a command. The Blockade works not through force, but through market consensus: if no insurer will underwrite a title and no buyer will accept it, the compact's economy seizes up without a single shot fired.

## Organic Resolution

The Liability State resolves inter-compact friction through a tiered mechanism that preserves private ordering while maintaining a backstop. At the first tier, private courts handle the dispute through standard liability law — the compact that caused harm compensates the compact that suffered harm. At the second tier, if the losing compact refuses to comply — a **Judicial Blockade** — its Good Faith Bond (10% of LVT escrowed at the Alliance level) is liquidated to cover the judgment. At the third tier, if the compact systematically defies rulings or violates the force prohibition, the Supreme Trust Oracle triggers the Trust Blockade — the ultimate enforcement mechanism that invalidates the rogue compact's Harberger land titles across the entire Alliance. This tiered approach means most disputes never reach the STO — the bond mechanism provides sufficient economic pressure for compliance without escalating to system-wide enforcement.

## Mitigation Strategies

| Mitigation | Description | Layer |
|---|---|---|
| **Private courts as default** | Inter-compact disputes go through standard private courts. Compacts agree on a neutral venue | Primary |
| **Good Faith Bonds** | 10% of LVT escrowed. Rulings paid from escrow. No confrontation | Secondary |
| **Supreme Trust Oracle** | Trust Blockade, Force Prohibition, Commons Enforcement | Backstop |
| **The Alliance Clause** | PMCs contractually forbidden from taking bounties that target Member Compact infrastructure except under Alliance-authorized actions | Structural |

## Bottom Line

| Risk | Primary Mitigation | Residual Risk |
|------|-------------------|---------------|
| Resource Spills | Private court liability action | Medium |
| Judicial Collapse | STO Trust Blockade | Low |
| Mercenary Civil War | Good Faith Bonds + Alliance Clause | Medium |
| Compact Refusal | Good Faith Bond liquidation | Low |

Without the STO as a backstop enforcement layer, the system risks the following failure mode: private courts work fine for routine disputes, but a rogue compact that refuses to participate, or a multilateral commons problem that no single court has standing over, creates a gap that can only be filled by a body with system-wide standing.
